Now everyone is talking about AI and its potential to reshape mathematics. 
Without adding my own opinion to this, I want to contribute by sharing how I am actually using it right now and what has changed since April. 
I have a lot of opinions on the different models, but I want to keep it brief.

- I still have a Gemini subscription, but the new version of Gemini (Gemini Flash) is not a frontier model. It is just really fast and the subscription API tokens are cheap.
- I pivoted to **Anthropic**. **Fable** is great, but too expensive to run. The new **Opus 5** is a mathematical workhorse for more than just routine tasks.
- **arXiv digest**: everyday I run a little AI-written script that fetches new preprints into a nice looking html page.
- **VSCode**: I switched LaTeX editor to VSCode and have an AI agent running on the side.
- **Live-TeXing**: I started live-TeXing with an AI agent, most recently for my [lecture notes from Lausanne](/assets/pdf/LectureNotesDanBE.pdf). In between, I tell it to 'fix compile'.
You can be really sloppy and also use macros that do not yet exist. It can also on the spot generate something like this, a pair-of-pants cobordism from $M$ to $N_1 \sqcup N_2$:

- **Counterexamples**: We are now all convinced that AI is good at that, in particular in fields with a large corpus of literature. Very helpful to check **edge cases** of your arguments.
- some convenient things that you can do now: Let AI **generate preamble** for your TeX documents; a polished preamble (and .bib file) are very pleasing. There is an endless number of ways to facilitate typing a TeX document with AI.
